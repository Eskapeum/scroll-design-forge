# Archived build observations

These are historical summaries from the skill's development, not current
instructions, approvals, performance benchmarks, or reservations of design
space. The artifacts have not been reverified by this document. Old ports are
not stable identities or destinations and are intentionally omitted.

Read only when a concrete design question benefits from comparison. The live
workflow is in [uniqueness.md](uniqueness.md); examples do not impose gates.

## What the early builds showed

Four unrelated early pages changed subject, imagery, and palette while
retaining much of the same full-bleed opening, fixed bar, pinned type, card
rail, and pinned CTA ending. The author's reported reaction was that they felt
like a template. That observation motivated separating structure from style;
it does not prove that any individual component is undesirable.

## Limits and useful lessons

The original records contained conflicting statements about which grammars were
"taken", aesthetic exceptions, and particular page lengths. Those claims were
not stable design constraints. This archive preserves the observed treatments
without carrying those commands into future work.

Reported lessons include:

- Repetition across unrelated briefs can be accidental; inspect composition.
- Deliberate repetition within one brand can strengthen recognition.
- A same-grammar variation can still be meaningfully distinct.
- A novel close gesture can obstruct the primary action; familiar controls can
  be the better ending.
- A continuous world adds seam, camera, and mobile-decoder risks worth proving
  before full production.
- Multiple hues and a grammar hybrid have both appeared in successful-looking
  historical directions, despite earlier universal bans.

These are hypotheses to examine in the current artifact, not guarantees of
quality or measured user response. No example grants permission to generate,
deploy, alter a registry, or reuse assets without rights.

For current comparisons, record the actual artifact identity, scope, observed
overlap, relevant differences, and the reason for intentional reuse. Do not
treat a grammar, palette, interaction family, or page-length band as exhausted.
